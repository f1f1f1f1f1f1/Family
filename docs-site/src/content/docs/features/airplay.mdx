# AirPlay

Family has an AirPlay screen. Mirror an iPhone, iPad or Mac to the display, or play music to it from any app with AirPlay, and the display shows the mirrored screen, or the cover and title of what's playing, with the sound.

> **On your Home Assistant machine's network.** The add-on's AirPlay receiver runs on the Home Assistant machine's own network, so iPhones, iPads and Macs on that network can find it. That's also why Home Assistant gives the add-on a lower security rating. Anyone on the network can send to it unless `airplay_password` is set, and `airplay: false` turns it off. Installing and updating the add-on take longer, since your Home Assistant machine builds the receiver (UxPlay) itself. AirPlay is only in the add-on, not the standalone Docker image. How it all works is in [`docs/airplay.md`](https://github.com/f1f1f1f1f1f1/Family/blob/main/docs/airplay.md).

---

## Sending to the display

- **A screen, from an iPhone or iPad:** open Control Center, tap **Screen Mirroring**, and choose **Family** (or the name set with `airplay_name`).
- **A screen, from a Mac:** Screen Mirroring is in Control Center too.
- **Music:** tap the AirPlay button in the app and choose **Family**.

If `airplay_password` is set, the device asks for it the first time.

---

## The AirPlay screen

While the add-on's receiver is on, the sidebar has an **AirPlay** icon after Photos (in the phone layout, it's under **More**).

- Like the photo frame, it covers the whole display. **Tap** to show the back arrow (top left); it hides again after 5 seconds. While nothing is being sent, the screen shows how to send to it, and the back arrow stays.
- For music, it shows the cover art, title, artist and album.
- Browsers keep the sound off until the page has been tapped. If the screen shows **Tap for sound**, tap anywhere.
- While it shows a mirrored screen or music, the display stays on and the screen saver waits.

---

## Opening by itself

**Settings > Display > Open AirPlay Automatically** (shown while the receiver is on, and on unless turned off):

- When a device starts sending, the display switches to the AirPlay screen. When it stops, the display goes back to the screen it was on, unless someone has moved on since.
- **Each display has its own choice.** Every display that opens the AirPlay screen plays the sound too, so with several displays, turn it off on the ones that shouldn't.
- Kid Displays never show it.

---

## Options

| Option | Default | What it does |
|--------|---------|--------------|
| `airplay` | `true` | `false` turns the receiver off |
| `airplay_name` | `Family` | The name devices show |
| `airplay_password` | none | A password devices must give (at least 4 characters) |

---

## Limitations

- It's new: it hasn't yet been tried with a real iPhone, iPad or Mac.
- On an iPhone or iPad used as the display, the mirrored screen needs iOS or iPadOS 17.1 or later. Before that, the sound still plays.
- The sound can be a little behind the picture.
- A video app's own AirPlay button, which hands over the video's web address rather than the screen, isn't supported: use Screen Mirroring instead.
- One device at a time: a second one takes over from the first.
